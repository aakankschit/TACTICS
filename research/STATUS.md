- **Legacy RWS broken on imbalanced libraries — thermal cycling is counterproductive**: On thrombin, Legacy RWS = 68.8 (worst method, -4.4 below Legacy Greedy). Root cause: Enhanced warmup creates 30:1 observation imbalance (148 obs/acid vs 5 obs/amine); fixed round-robin thermal cycling cools amines with already-loose posteriors, flattening Boltzmann selection → duplicate combinations → early search termination. GMIC-weighted rotation inverts this (+9.4 pts), but both TACTICS TT-TS and RWS still inherit the 30:1 posterior std imbalance from Enhanced warmup.
- **Enhanced warmup is universally optimal — GMIC exploits the information asymmetry it creates**: On thrombin (130×3844), Balanced warmup significantly *hurts*: Balanced-TT-TS = 72.5 vs Enhanced-TT-TS = 78.0 (-5.5, p<0.0001), Balanced-RWS = 73.5 vs Enhanced-RWS = 78.5 (-5.0, p<0.0001). Enhanced-Greedy = 73.2 ≈ Balanced-Greedy = 74.4 (ns). Mechanism: Enhanced warmup's stochastic parallel pairing naturally over-samples the small component (147 obs/acid vs 5 obs/amine), pre-solving acid ranking. GMIC detects the differential posterior quality and redirects search to the unsolved component (amines). Without GMIC (greedy), the asymmetry is inert. With unguided thermal cycling (Legacy RWS = 66.7), the asymmetry is actively harmful — round-robin heating/cooling is maximally miscalibrated. On balanced libraries, Enhanced warmup wins via data efficiency (all products unique, no duplicates). 7 methods, 20 replicates, 29,220 eval budget.
- **TT-TS is robust to docking failures — adenine library (17% NaN rate, 3-comp)**: Adenine (29 amidines × 688 isocyanides × 294 aldehydes, 4.9M scored / 5.9M theoretical, FRED Chemgauss4 minimize). TT-TS = 93.9 ± 13.9 (19/20 reps at 97, one outlier at 35). TACTICS RWS = 71.8 ± 27.6 (bimodal: ~94-97 or ~29-34). Legacy RWS = 40.1 ± 23.8 (bimodal: ~75-88 or ~24-29). Greedy methods plateau at ~31-32 (std ~1). All pairwise TACTICS vs Legacy comparisons p < 0.0001, Cohen's d 0.96–4.53 (large). TT-TS vs TACTICS RWS: +22.1, d=0.67 (medium), p=0.001. Mechanism: 17% of products fail docking (NaN scores), reducing per-reagent observations during warmup. Failures are uniformly distributed across reagents (80–92% coverage, one outlier amidine_003 at 40.7%), preserving relative SAR rankings. TT-TS's two-sample disagreement mechanism inherently targets under-observed reagents (wide posteriors → high disagreement), making it robust to information loss. RWS thermal cycling catches this intermittently (bimodality). Greedy ignores it entirely.
- **Uniform vs concentrated docking failures — implications for TACTICS reliability**: The adenine failure distribution is approximately uniform across reagents, meaning the SAR signal is preserved despite 17% information loss. This is the benign case — TACTICS recovers hits reliably. If failures were concentrated in a specific chemical subclass (e.g., all bulky aldehydes fail), that would create a systematic SAR blind spot undetectable from posteriors alone. GMIC per-component trajectories could serve as a diagnostic: anomalous convergence patterns (premature stabilization with low GMIC) may indicate concentrated failures. This is an open question for the library diagnostics paper (Paper 3).

With this many observations, posterior means become informative proxies for "this reagent tends to participate in good combinations" even when the underlying mechanism is interaction-driven (non-additive). Riley's 10× penalty is expected to emerge at lower budgets (0.1–0.5%) where per-reagent observations are insufficient for posteriors to converge. Both ROCS and docking have a large enough budget at 2% that the non-additivity issue is overcome. **Fixing the rotation-evaluation ordering and batch-granularity CATS adaptation should produce modest to medium improvement in TACTICS RWS recovery on 3-component libraries.**

**Observations motivating this hypothesis (2026-04-04/05)**:

1. **Rotation-evaluation ordering is wrong**: In `sampler.search()`, `rotate_component_weighted()` (step 2) runs BEFORE `evaluate_batch()` and posterior updates (step 5). The rotation decision for cycle N+1 uses posteriors from cycle N-1 — the current batch's 100 evaluations are not incorporated. The rotation GMIC is one batch stale. Cycle-to-cycle GMIC changes average 0.008 (0.019 in early search where the lag matters most).

2. **CATS multiplier is effectively noise on balanced 3-comp libraries**: Median multiplier = 1.000, P5-P95 range = [0.90, 1.17]. CATS contributes ~10% of the total temperature differential between heated and cooled components. Rotation (which component gets alpha vs beta) creates a 13× larger shift in Boltzmann selection probability than CATS at P90. The multiplier fluctuates cycle-to-cycle without developing persistent trends because it responds to instantaneous relative_gmic that barely changes per batch (std = 0.12–0.16 on these libraries).

3. **Batch granularity suppresses CATS responsiveness**: CATS gets 195 update opportunities (one per cycle) across the entire search. All 100 compounds in a batch use identical temperatures. The divergence gate fires on 33% of early iterations (where adaptation would be most valuable) because batch updates produce larger posterior jumps than the per-iteration updates the gate was designed for.

4. **GMIC-weighted rotation IS working but the effect is small**: Most-flexible component heated 35.6–36.5% of the time vs 33.3% round-robin. This translates to +4–6 extra heating rounds (400–600 compounds) directed to the right component. On betti, the most-flexible component gets 69.6 heating rounds vs 60.6 for most-critical (round-robin: 65 each).

**Approach**: Two separable changes: (a) move `rotate_component_weighted` after evaluation/posterior update so the rotation decision for cycle N+1 uses cycle N's posteriors, (b) replace instantaneous relative_gmic with EMA-smoothed relative GMIC that accumulates directional signal across cycles. Test with 3-comp ROCS benchmark (10 libraries, 109 queries, 20 replicates): current vs ordering fix vs ordering fix + EMA CATS.

**Expected outcome**: Modest to medium improvement in recovery. Libraries with growing inter-component GMIC spread (poparov: 0.162→0.533, betti: 0.088→0.318, passerini: 0.150→0.397) are most likely to benefit. Libraries with uniformly easy SAR (orru, dobener) will not improve because the problem is absence of inter-component differential, not mechanism responsiveness. A null result would confirm that GMIC-weighted rotation alone is the full mechanism and CATS should be simplified or removed for batch processing.

**Previous state (2026-04-02)**: Mechanism analysis and benchmark validation were complete. Per-query diagnostic analysis had confirmed GMIC adaptively detects which components need exploration. The smaller 3-comp advantage was attributed to easier SAR. This session's deeper temperature decomposition revealed that the CATS multiplier is not contributing meaningfully on these libraries due to batch processing constraints, refining the previous assessment that "CATS provides meaningful temperature modulation."

## Open Questions

<!--
  Questions that emerged from research but haven't been investigated yet.
  These may become future hypotheses in new worktrees.
-->

- How does the observation gate behave in the high-N regime?
- Optimal transition point from James-Stein shrinkage to empirical variance?
- MOTS/TSEMO extension: what is the right surrogate for multi-objective TS over combinatorial libraries?
- Direct uncertainty-driven diversity sampling: replace thermal cycling entirely with a mechanism that tracks per-reagent observation counts or posterior uncertainty and selects reagents proportional to their information gap. This would bypass thermal cycling's failure modes on imbalanced libraries and operate on the selection lever directly rather than indirectly via temperature perturbation. Distinct from the dead-ended auxiliary regression approach because it modifies the selection policy, not a secondary model.
- Can GMIC per-component trajectories diagnose concentrated docking failures? If failures are concentrated in a chemical subclass, the affected component may show premature GMIC stabilization with artificially low criticality. This would provide a warning that the search may be missing a region of chemical space. Testable with synthetic failure injection on the adenine dataset. Feeds into Paper 3 (library diagnostics).
- How does TT-TS bimodality on adenine relate to warmup quality? **Root cause identified (2026-03-21)**: Rep 15 failure fully explained by amidine_003 under-exploration — 62/65 missed compounds belong to amidine_003 (68/100 top-100 hits concentrated in one reagent). Stochastic warmup partner pairing produced an uninformative posterior for amidine_003 (mediocre AND narrow), so TT-TS disagreement never triggered exploration (0.6% search budget vs 4–15% in 19 successful reps). Rep 15 recovered 35/35 non-amidine_003 top hits — it found everything it could given the blind spot. **Open for solution**: can warmup be made robust to extreme hit concentration? Options: adaptive warmup rounds for uncertain reagents, post-warmup posterior quality check, or minimum-exploration guarantees per reagent.
- **Mechanism visualization plots — DONE (2026-04-02)**: Marimo notebook `tutorials/diagnostic_benchmark_plots.py` renders all 21 diagnostic figures (10 RWS + 10 TT-TS + 1 min_GMIC scatter). Three plot functions added to `diagnostic_plots.py`: `plot_rws_diagnostic` (dual-axis GMIC + temperature with divergence gate shading, 95% CI, rolling average), `plot_ttts_diagnostic` (dual-axis disagreement EMA + heated_scale with threshold bands, 95% CI), `plot_min_gmic_vs_advantage` (scatter with SAR difficulty zones). Remaining: per-reagent rank bump charts and anytime recovery curves require additional per-iteration tracking not yet implemented.
- **min_GMIC as a prescriptive diagnostic**: min_GMIC < 1.0 predicts where GMIC-directed exploration provides value. Could be computed from warmup posteriors alone (before search begins) to estimate expected TACTICS advantage and guide method selection. Connects to Paper 3 (library diagnostics).
- **Continuous GMIC-based temperature gradient (follow-up study)**: Replace binary heated/cooled assignment with per-component temperature interpolated from GMIC: T_c = beta + (alpha - beta) * (flexibility_c / max_flexibility). Most flexible component gets alpha, least flexible gets beta, middle components get proportional temperatures instead of being forced into beta. Preserves explore/exploit contrast while giving intermediate components appropriate exploration levels. Key constraint from diagnostic analysis: the explore/exploit contrast itself drives gains (2-comp libraries with both GMIC < 1.0 still benefit from single-component heating), so any multi-component scheme must maintain at least one fully-cooled component. Diagnostic data shows 2+ components below GMIC 1.0 is rare on 3-comp (3% of query-phases), so the practical impact may be limited to specific SAR landscapes.
- **GMIC noise denominator inflated by unexplored reagents (Paper 3)**: GMIC's `mean(σᵢ²)` is dominated by the ~85-90% of reagents that remain at warmup-level observation counts after directed search. Noise is 2-12× higher than expected with uniform observations (measured across 3-comp ROCS libraries). This makes GMIC a conservative, blunted signal that underestimates the true SNR of the exploited reagent subpopulation. Conservatism is why GMIC self-corrects and never underperforms round-robin, but recovery is likely lower than achievable with a sharper metric. **Proposed solutions**: (1) observation-weighted GMIC variant (weight σᵢ² by observation count or filter to reagents above a minimum obs threshold), (2) diagnostic plot showing unique reagent usage fraction per component per iteration (reveals how quickly the search narrows and whether narrowing differs across components — first-pass assessment of whether observation-weighted GMIC would differ), (3) filtered GMIC computed on top-K reagents by observation count. **Manuscript context**: discussion point for Paper 1 (TACTICS outperforms legacy even with conservative estimates; recovering 2-3 additional compounds per 100 is meaningful), primary investigation for Paper 3 (does sharper GMIC translate to better recovery, or does conservatism serve as beneficial regularization?). <!-- heptabase: [OPEN QUESTION] GMIC Noise Denominator Inflated by Unexplored Reagents — Observation-Weighted Variant -->
