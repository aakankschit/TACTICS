- **Enhanced warmup is universally optimal — GMIC exploits the information asymmetry it creates**: On thrombin (130×3844), Balanced warmup significantly *hurts*: Balanced-TT-TS = 72.5 vs Enhanced-TT-TS = 78.0 (-5.5, p<0.0001), Balanced-RWS = 73.5 vs Enhanced-RWS = 78.5 (-5.0, p<0.0001). Mechanism: Enhanced warmup's stochastic parallel pairing naturally over-samples the small component (147 obs/acid vs 5 obs/amine), pre-solving acid ranking. RWS thermal cycling catches this intermittently (bimodality). Greedy ignores it entirely.
- **Uniform vs concentrated docking failures — implications for TACTICS reliability**: The adenine failure distribution is approximately uniform across reagents, meaning the SAR signal is preserved despite 17% information loss. This is the benign case — TACTICS recovers hits reliably. If failures were concentrated in a specific chemical subclass (e.g., all bulky aldehydes fail), that would create a systematic SAR blind spot undetectable from posteriors alone. GMIC per-component trajectories could serve as a diagnostic: anomalous convergence patterns (premature stabilization with low GMIC) may indicate concentrated failures. - **Docking vs ROCS recovery comparable at 2% budget — Riley's 10× penalty not observed**: At 2% evaluation budget, docking libraries recover at rates comparable to ROCS (amide 97.2, quinazoline 85.2, thrombin 81.0 vs ROCS mean ~86). Riley's 10× penalty is expected to emerge at lower budgets (0.1–0.5%) where per-reagent observations are insufficient for posteriors to converge. Both ROCS and docking have a large enough budget at 2% that the non-additivity issue is overcome. - **Layer-2 diagnostic — within-component adaptive exploration intensity (CATS multiplier / disagreement)**: extend the GMIC signal→decision→result grammar one level down. RWS: `ema_relative_gmic` (signal) → `cats_multiplier` (decision, Boltzmann temperature) → within-component sampling breadth (result); TT-TS: `disagreement_ema` → `heated_scale` (σ-inflation) → within-component sampling breadth. Planned function `plot_adaptive_intensity`.
- How does the observation gate behave in the high-N regime?
- Optimal transition point from James-Stein shrinkage to empirical variance?
- MOTS/TSEMO extension: what is the right surrogate for multi-objective TS over combinatorial libraries?
- Direct uncertainty-driven diversity sampling: replace thermal cycling entirely with a mechanism that tracks per-reagent observation counts or posterior uncertainty and selects reagents proportional to their information gap. This would bypass thermal cycling's failure modes on imbalanced libraries and operate on the selection lever directly rather than indirectly via temperature perturbation. Distinct from the dead-ended auxiliary regression approach because it modifies the selection policy, not a secondary model.
- Can GMIC per-component trajectories diagnose concentrated docking failures? If failures are concentrated in a chemical subclass, the affected component may show premature GMIC stabilization with artificially low criticality. This would provide a warning that the search may be missing a region of chemical space. Testable with synthetic failure injection on the adenine dataset. Feeds into Paper 3 (library diagnostics).
- How does TT-TS bimodality on adenine relate to warmup quality? **Root cause identified (2026-03-21)**: Rep 15 failure fully explained by amidine_003 under-exploration — 62/65 missed compounds belong to amidine_003 (68/100 top-100 hits concentrated in one reagent). Stochastic warmup partner pairing produced an uninformative posterior for amidine_003 (mediocre AND narrow), so TT-TS disagreement never triggered exploration (0.6% search budget vs 4–15% in 19 successful reps). Rep 15 recovered 35/35 non-amidine_003 top hits — it found everything it could given the blind spot. **Open for solution**: can warmup be made robust to extreme hit concentration? Options: adaptive warmup rounds for uncertain reagents, post-warmup posterior quality check, or minimum-exploration guarantees per reagent.
- **Mechanism visualization plots — DONE (2026-04-02)**: Marimo notebook `tutorials/diagnostic_benchmark_plots.py` renders all 21 diagnostic figures (10 RWS + 10 TT-TS + 1 min_GMIC scatter). Three plot functions added to `diagnostic_plots.py`: `plot_rws_diagnostic` (dual-axis GMIC + temperature with divergence gate shading, 95% CI, rolling average), `plot_ttts_diagnostic` (dual-axis disagreement EMA + heated_scale with threshold bands, 95% CI), `plot_min_gmic_vs_advantage` (scatter with SAR difficulty zones). Remaining: per-reagent rank bump charts and anytime recovery curves require additional per-iteration tracking not yet implemented.
- **min_GMIC as a prescriptive diagnostic**: min_GMIC < 1.0 predicts where GMIC-directed exploration provides value. Could be computed from warmup posteriors alone (before search begins) to estimate expected TACTICS advantage and guide method selection. Connects to Paper 3 (library diagnostics).
- **Continuous GMIC-based temperature gradient (follow-up study)**: Replace binary heated/cooled assignment with per-component temperature interpolated from GMIC: T_c = beta + (alpha - beta) * (flexibility_c / max_flexibility). Most flexible component gets alpha, least flexible gets beta, middle components get proportional temperatures instead of being forced into beta. Preserves explore/exploit contrast while giving intermediate components appropriate exploration levels. Key constraint from diagnostic analysis: the explore/exploit contrast itself drives gains (2-comp libraries with both GMIC < 1.0 still benefit from single-component heating), so any multi-component scheme must maintain at least one fully-cooled component. Diagnostic data shows 2+ components below GMIC 1.0 is rare on 3-comp (3% of query-phases), so the practical impact may be limited to specific SAR landscapes.
- **GMIC noise denominator inflated by unexplored reagents (Paper 3)**: GMIC's `mean(σᵢ²)` is dominated by the ~85-90% of reagents that remain at warmup-level observation counts after directed search. Noise is 2-12× higher than expected with uniform observations (measured across 3-comp ROCS libraries). This makes GMIC a conservative, blunted signal that underestimates the true SNR of the exploited reagent subpopulation. Conservatism is why GMIC self-corrects and never underperforms round-robin, but recovery is likely lower than achievable with a sharper metric. **Manuscript context**: discussion point for Paper 1 (TACTICS outperforms legacy even with conservative estimates; recovering 2-3 additional compounds per 100 is meaningful); proposed solutions (observation-weighted GMIC variant, filtered GMIC, reagent usage diagnostics) scoped to Paper 1b (TACTICS v2) — see Future Manuscript Pipeline. <!-- heptabase: [OPEN QUESTION] GMIC Noise Denominator Inflated by Unexplored Reagents — Observation-Weighted Variant -->
- **Simultaneous multi-component exploration (future method improvement / candidate spin-off)**: All current methods (Legacy RWS, TACTICS RWS, TACTICS TT-TS) heat exactly ONE component per cycle (`is_heated = component_idx == self.current_component_idx` in `roulette_wheel.py:261` and `top_two_selection.py:131`), so a single combination never explores two components at once. This is a SHARED limitation — NOT something TACTICS improves over Legacy RWS (GMIC only redirects the single heated slot; it never heats more than one). A true multi-component scheme (≥2 components heated simultaneously, retaining ≥1 fully-cooled component to preserve the explore/exploit contrast) could help hidden-hub libraries whose hits require the conjunction of two mean-hidden reagents (where one-at-a-time heating must discover them sequentially). Closely tied to the continuous GMIC temperature-gradient question above. Candidate for a `/spinout` investigation or a Paper 1b (TACTICS v2) method refinement. (Surfaced 2026-06-04 while correcting the Established Result that wrongly claimed TACTICS does coordinated multi-component exploration.)
